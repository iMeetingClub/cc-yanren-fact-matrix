Free Discussion 2: Community platform UI and backend redesign
Facts
1. The goal is to connect “digital identities” with real offline communities.
2. The current UI concept includes a card/flip-card and optional identity disclosure at admission.
3. Camera-based recognition is proposed for shared/public areas (e.g., restrooms/cleaning, kitchen/fridge, fields); direct API integration may be unavailable and may require a local recognition computer.
4. Field management can be implemented simply by generating tasks from what cameras can see (e.g., pulling weeds from tea plants; watering when plants look dried out).
5. AI planting recommendations were cited as potentially unreliable, with a story describing plants dying and causing financial loss.
Judgments
1. Building the community “foundation” (atmosphere/soil) is difficult but determines the quality of human expression.
2. Tools can support cultivating the foundation, but the system should eventually step back once the foundation is strong.
3. AI guidance for planting may be less reliable than recording concrete observable “things.”
4. Local infrastructure may be needed because direct camera/API integration may not be available.
Opinions
1. Interaction between people is the most important part; tools should assist rather than replace community relationships.
2. A real-first approach is preferable: data analysis is secondary to how people relate.
3. The most important farming value is embodiment and daily participation.
4. Start from the smallest, most urgent, achievable point and expand iteratively.
Suggestions
1. Use tools to help people form a habit: when someone notices something, the tool prompts them to show up and interact better.
2. Begin field management by recording concrete planting actions/inputs (e.g., weeds pulling, watering) rather than relying on AI “how-to-plant” advice.
3. Prototype feasibility may require using existing cooperative cameras plus a local recognition computer when API access is unavailable.
4. Test kitchen/fridge recognition to see whether camera data can reliably support “what was put in + who did it,” within the privacy approach.
5. Start with the simplest possible use case (e.g., tea-plant visibility leading to basic tasks) and iterate from there.
